# What happens to a program that touches a hardware port

These theorems join two earlier stories into one: the port policy that denies a user program any direct access to hardware ports, and the fault handler that cleans up after a program that misbehaves. Together they show the full consequence of a program poking at a port: the poke is refused with every device untouched, the offending program is permanently retired with all its memory reclaimed, and a peer program is handed the processor instead. Nothing the attacker controls, not the port number, the value, or any of its saved registers, has any influence over what happens next or who runs next.

- `containDeniedPort_total` — Bookkeeping: the combined deny-then-clean-up step always produces some definite outcome on every possible input; it can never get stuck.
- `denied_port_contained` — The headline guarantee: under the accepted, freshly confirmed settings, a program's port attempt is denied with every device byte unchanged, and that program is fully retired, no longer alive or runnable, out of the ready queue and the current slot, stripped of its saved state, with every address space and memory mapping it owned torn down.
- `untrusted_words_cannot_select` — The words the attacker supplies, port number, value, size, cannot ever be stamped kernel-approved on the device side, and swapping one set of words for another changes nothing at all about who is cleaned up or who runs next.
- `attacker_registers_cannot_relabel` — The attacker's saved processor registers are simply not an input to this step: erasing them or substituting any other values yields the identical outcome, byte for byte.
- `denied_attempt_cannot_return_to_faulting` — A program whose port attempt was denied can never get the processor back: after any successful cleanup it is not the current program, waits in no queue, and has no saved state to resume from.
- `witness_serial_denied_and_dispatched` — A concrete worked example with two programs: program A's write to the serial port is denied with the devices spotless, A is retired completely, and program B becomes the running program.
- `witness_untrusted_probe_independent` — In that same example it makes no difference whether A pokes the serial console, the debug-exit port, or the interrupt controller: the devices end identical and the handover to B is identical in all three cases.
- `denied_port_contained_nonvacuous` — A demonstration that the headline theorem is about something real: its conditions genuinely hold in the concrete two-program example, where the poke is denied and the offender is retired.
