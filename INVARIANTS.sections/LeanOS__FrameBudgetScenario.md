# A worked story: two programs sharing memory safely

This file plays out one complete, machine-checked story with two programs, A and B. A fills its one-frame budget and is refused more; B allocates from its own budget unaffected; A is then terminated (or, in a second story line, releases its frame voluntarily); and B's next allocation reuses A's old physical frame — but only after the kernel has flushed its address caches and wiped every byte A left behind, and A's old access handle is refused as stale. The theorems check every step of this story against the full budget and scrubbing machinery, including the rule that a reclaimed frame becomes visible only after the exact cache-flush acknowledgement it was tied to.

- `retirement_prepare_keeps_frame_unallocatable` — Preparing to retire A's frame publishes nothing early: the frame stays owned, A's mapping stays in place, and no fresh allocation can slip in while the retirement is pending.
- `retirement_wrong_ack_stutters` — A flush acknowledgement token issued for the voluntary-release step can never confirm the termination step, even though both steps require the same kind of full cache flush; the wrong token changes nothing.
- `retirement_ack_publishes_reclaimed_frame` — The exact matching acknowledgement is the first moment the reclaimed frame and freed budget become visible — and at that moment A's leftover byte is still sitting in the frame, awaiting the wipe that happens at reallocation.
- `invalidation_acknowledged_fresh_republication` — The full path is prepare, then the exact acknowledgement, then fresh allocation: B's new mapping cannot exist in any published state before the acknowledgement, and when it appears the reused frame has been wiped clean in its entirety.
- `canonical_dispatch` — Bookkeeping: for every legal step of the script, the compact numeric dispatch table used by the machine returns exactly the reply word the script prescribes.
- `step_refinement` — Every step of the abstract script is exactly a run of the full detailed model: the simple state machine and the real budget and scrubbing machinery always agree.
- `a_retry_is_budgetExhausted_and_unchanged` — A's second allocation attempt is refused as over budget, and the refusal changes nothing at all.
- `peer_allocation_remains_available` — With A completely out of budget, B's allocation from its own budget still succeeds.
- `termination_restores_exactly_one_a_frame` — Terminating A frees exactly A's frame — its usage drops from one to zero and its physical frame returns to the pool — while B's frame stays owned, and A's leftover marker byte is at that moment still in the freed frame.
- `fresh_b_lifetime_and_stale_a_denial` — After B's fresh allocation on the reused frame, the new object carries a never-before-used identity, the frame's contents read as wiped at both ends, and A's old handle is refused as stale.
- `machine_mapping_is_authoritative_and_reuses_frame` — The user-visible page mapping follows the kernel's records exactly: A's mapping exists while A owns the frame, vanishes at termination, and B's fresh mapping reuses the same physical frame at the kernel-chosen page.
- `retirement_effects_are_exact_and_trace_bound` — Termination and voluntary release are the only two steps that retire a frame; both demand a full cache flush, and each one's authorization token is bound to its own step and can never be replayed for the other.
- `fresh_publication_follows_authoritative_termination_flush` — B's fresh object and its new handle are reachable only through the exact termination step whose token demands the full flush.
- `fresh_b_publication_is_completely_scrubbed` — The reclaimed physical frame is wiped in its entirety before B's new object and its fresh handle become observable.
- `exact_reclaimed_frame_scrubbed_before_fresh_generation` — Following the one physical frame through the whole story: A's leftover byte remains visible in the model right up until allocation atomically wipes the frame and publishes B's object under a never-reused identity.
- `release_and_repeated_release_are_checked` — In the voluntary story line, releasing A's frame frees it in both the budget and content records, and trying to release it a second time is refused as stale without changing anything.
- `canonical_sequence_complete` — Running the whole eight-step termination script from the start really does reach the finished state.
- `release_sequence_complete` — Running the alternate four-step voluntary-release script from the start also reaches its finished state.
- `canonical_sequence_adjacent` — Bookkeeping: any script that runs to completion does so one legal step at a time — its first command is a legal move, and the remainder completes from the state that move produces.
